# Can Consistent Forces Recover Molecular Shapes?

Group 9, 11-785 Introduction to Deep Learning, Carnegie Mellon University: Shedrack Eze, Olufunke Abodunrin, Emmanuel Aaron and Muhammed Idris. Updated 26 November 2026 with the final results.

## Project overview

We study whether training a direct-force model to agree with its own energy gradient gives forces that lead a geometry search back to the correct stable shape of a molecule. We use aspirin (21 atoms) from Landscape17, where each connection joins a transition state to two minima through two downhill branches, and we split its 37 connections at random into 26 for training, 5 for validation and 6 for testing (4,891, 974 and 1,127 records) so that both branches of a connection always stay together. All E3x models share one backbone, and we compare an energy-derived model, a direct force head and consistency training at strengths 0.1, 1 and 10, with a larger MACE model as an energy-derived reference. A search counts as a recovery when its largest force component falls to 0.01 eV/Å or below and the final shape lies within 0.05 Å of the target minimum after symmetry-aware alignment.

## Results

Consistency training at strength 0.1 matched direct prediction on validation force error, at 38.0 against 38.2 meV/Å, and it cut the disagreement between the two force outputs by more than two orders of magnitude for about twice the training time. Strength 1 gave the lowest mean E3x force error at 34.7 meV/Å, while MACE reached 5.3 meV/Å.

Our original force-following search recovered none of the ten validation starts for any model we tested, including runs with per-atom movement caps and, for the energy-derived models, an energy-decrease check. Its step size kept doubling without an energy check, and 213 of its 250 primary runs used the whole budget of 1,500 force requests. We then repeated the test with standard ASE optimizers, which stop on the largest per-atom force norm (fmax) where the recovery rule uses the largest single force component, and we ran each search to fmax 0.01 eV/Å and then on to 0.001 eV/Å. A per-atom norm of 0.01 eV/Å keeps every component at or below 0.01 eV/Å, so these endpoints are scored with the same recovery rule. On the same ten starts MACE recovered 7 and then 9 with L-BFGS, 8 and then 9 with BFGS, and 0 and then 5 with FIRE. This shows that the custom search limited recovery for MACE on these starts, but it does not explain the E3x results, since the E3x models still recovered none of the original starts with L-BFGS or FIRE.

For the energy-derived E3x model, L-BFGS and FIRE both recovered 0 of the 10 original starts at both stopping levels, and its six known-minimum controls depend on the search and the stopping level. With L-BFGS at fmax 0.01 eV/Å the six controls converged 0.056 to 0.33 Å from their reference minima, so none met the 0.05 Å rule. With FIRE at fmax 0.01 eV/Å they ended 0.009 to 0.160 Å away and 4 of 6 matched, because FIRE stopped soon after leaving the start, while continuing FIRE to 0.001 eV/Å moved all six to 0.076 to 0.254 Å and none matched. This suggests that the model's own minima sit slightly away from the reference minima and match only when a search stops close to where it started.

None of the six direct and consistency checkpoints converged with L-BFGS or FIRE, from the original starts or from the known minima. Counting the ten original starts and six known minima for each checkpoint, 95 of their 96 L-BFGS runs and 76 of their 96 FIRE runs ended with the molecule broken apart, meaning the atoms no longer formed one group when linked within 5 Å. This means that agreement on the training data was not enough to make the direct forces usable for finding minima in these tests, so the clearest next step is to apply the consistency penalty on the structures a search actually visits.

## Limitations

These results come from two seeds per E3x setting, a single MACE seed and one molecule, so they are descriptive. The five validation connections served both for checkpoint selection and for the recovery tests, and all six target minima also appear in the training connections, so the recovery test measures how well a model reaches known destinations. The six test connections were never scored.
