# Scalar and equivariant graph representations: study specification

20 October 2026. Proposed extension to the completed force-formulation study. This specification is for review; no implementation, timing probe, training or paid allocation has run for this study. It does not change the midterm report, historical results, main-test status or teammate ownership.

## Question and interpretation

At a fixed training recipe, data exposure and approximately matched parameter count, does adding vector-valued equivariant internal graph features change validation force error and computational cost compared with scalar-only invariant graph features? Both arms predict a rotation-invariant scalar energy, and both obtain rotation-equivariant forces by differentiation. This is **not** equivariant versus nonequivariant force output, nor neural network versus graph network. Both are graph neural networks. No generic novelty or universal model-superiority claim is proposed.

The primary contrast is E64 versus SP below. S64 is a secondary same-width control. Differences in SP's width mean the primary contrast concerns representations at similar parameter budgets, not an isolated causal effect of symmetry. The controls jointly expose width/parameter differences. It is a fixed-recipe comparison, not a claim about each architecture's optimally tuned performance. The recipe was selected on the equivariant energy model, so the scalar models may be disadvantaged; disclose this rather than retune after seeing results.

## Notebook and resume behavior

Deliver a short end-to-end notebook with configuration, environment/data preparation, and run/resume/progress cells. No manual READY_FOR_REVIEW barrier, alarm confirmation, arbitrary six-hour pause or permanent owner receipt. Numerical launch scope is reviewed before the notebook is handed over; normal disconnect recovery must not require another bespoke recovery script.

Use one training process/runtime at a time for this study. Full checkpoint state includes parameters, optimizer state, update count, sampler/model RNG, normalization, BEST payload/update and validation prefix. Save a complete Drive generation at each5,000-update boundary or first update after10 elapsed minutes, whichever comes first, and at normal termination; save current BEST promptly. Stage locally, hash bytes, copy uniquely named generation, and write a small completion receipt last. Preserve historical generations. On restart scan small receipts, verify/unpack only the latest complete candidate, and resume the next update. Ignore incomplete writes with a recorded reason; if the latest committed candidate is corrupt, record rejection before trying the prior complete one. Do not scan/re-hash every historical payload. A local process lock releases on process death; never leave a persistent stale-owner block. Detect obvious duplicate commits/update collisions and stop that writer; do not delete another runtime's files. Clearly display that only one production runtime may be active.

Identity for admission is numerical source hashes, model/objective/config, data/normalization and dependency identities, not a documentation commit, wall clock, Drive account name or exact GPU/driver string. Log the latter as execution metadata. Every complete checkpoint stores two probes built on copies, without advancing the live optimizer or sampler: (a) energy/force predictions for the first four training records in original prepared order; (b) one next optimizer step on the batch obtained from a copy of the saved sampler RNG. Save all record IDs, input arrays/labels, predicted arrays and expected post-step parameter/optimizer leaves, update/step counters, model RNG and canonical next sampler state. Probe construction time is included in s_c. On reload, first authenticate source/objective/data identities, full-state hashes, named-leaf shapes and dtypes. Compare fixed-record energies/forces at `atol=1e-8,rtol=1e-6`; compare floating post-step parameter/optimizer leaves at `atol=1e-10,rtol=1e-6`; require exact record IDs, integer counters, RNG and canonical sampler state. Reject nonfinite outputs. Byte-check that the loaded live state is unchanged after probes. Historical or future global BEST generations ahead of the restored update are never adopted into that state's validation prefix; use only its authenticated saved control. If a tolerance fails, retain the checkpoint and mismatch record for review without modifying state or automatically increasing tolerances. The same probe runs when the runtime restarts, including a device change; a compatible GPU is not rejected solely for its name. A migrated segment is disclosed and excluded from strict identical-hardware timing. If migration occurs, score all endpoints on one shared verified device before comparing error and report training-cost segments separately; do not claim a clean matched-hardware cost comparison from mixed devices.

Use existing checkpoint primitives where suitable, without introducing a new service/lease framework. Preserve historical checkpoints and source; do not normalize their identity rules in place. Progress prints during installation, compilation, checkpoint selection and training. A fatal setup/numerical error saves evidence and stops the affected operation; normal server interruption resumes completed work instead of retraining from zero. Hardware-rate or forecasting uncertainty must not become a wall of interactive notebook confirmations.
