\begin{abstract}
Learned molecular forces, which describe pushes and pulls on atoms, can reduce the cost of searching for stable three-dimensional molecular shapes. However, accurate predictions on stored examples do not guarantee recovery of the correct shape. This study compares models that calculate forces from negative energy slopes, predict forces directly, or train direct forces to agree with energy-derived forces on aspirin. Validation force errors and computing costs are measured separately, and five saved model states are tested from ten shared starting shapes whose targets also occur in training. Direct and consistency-trained models meet the declared error-matching rule at one penalty strength, but all five tested states, including the energy baseline, recover zero of ten targets. Force errors at reference shapes and excessive coordinate steps leave the recovery question unresolved; this evaluation cannot rank the methods by recovery success.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Repeated calculations of molecular energies and forces are expensive, so learned approximations can reduce simulation cost. Training changes a model's adjustable numbers, called parameters, to match reference examples.

Energy is a number assigned to an atomic arrangement. A stretched spring gives a useful picture: stretching it further stores more energy, and its pull points toward lower energy. Energy slope means how quickly energy changes when an atom moves; the energy baseline calculates forces from the negative slopes. The direct model predicts force arrows themselves, while consistency training penalizes disagreement with forces calculated from negative energy slopes. Direct prediction skips the slope calculation and can be cheaper, but its forces need not follow any single energy.

The objective is to compare correct-shape recovery at comparable force error, with computing cost measured separately. Recovery requires the search to reach its assigned target shape and meet the force stopping rule; neither condition alone establishes success.
\section{Literature Review}
Landscape17 supplies molecular shapes, reference forces and paths between stable arrangements~\cite{landscape}. It motivates checking final shapes beyond prediction errors, while Bigi et al. describe problems when direct forces do not follow one energy function~\cite{bigi}. Together, these studies motivate separating a force-based stop from structural success.

The models are built with E3x, software for networks that obey three-dimensional symmetry rules~\cite{e3x}. MACE supplies a separate established energy-derived model for predicting energies and forces~\cite{mace}. Earlier work already studies physical constraints in training losses~\cite{takamoto}. The study compares error, cost and recovery under shared conditions; consistency training itself is not new.

\section{Data and Preprocessing}
The dataset contains aspirin from Landscape17~\cite{landscape}, with 21 atoms in each molecule. Reference energies and forces come from hybrid density-functional theory, a detailed calculation based on electron physics, so these reference labels are calculated answers rather than exact experimental truth.

A transition state is a high-energy arrangement with two downhill branches toward stable arrangements, called minima. A connection group contains both branches' records, which stay in the same partition to avoid separating the two sides of one transition state. Training adjusts parameters, validation selects settings and saved models, and the test partition is reserved for later trained-model evaluation.

The training set contains 26 connections and 4,891 records. Validation contains five connections and 974 records; testing contains six connections and 1,127 records. Training-only normalization puts energy and force errors on comparable scales, while shared minima mean that records are not independent examples of new destinations. Each branch targets the minimum it should reach; empirical classifications corrected 26 of 74 targets previously assigned from filename ordering. Earlier model-free software checks parsed test coordinates and labels but did not choose models or solver settings; no trained model has been scored on the test partition.
\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Model Architecture}
The E3x models are graph neural networks in which atoms are nodes and nearby atom pairs form links. Successive calculation stages, called layers, pass learned information along these links. Inputs are atom types and three-dimensional positions; outputs are one molecular energy and a force arrow for each atom. The three compared model types use 64 feature channels containing learned numbers and vectors, two message-passing rounds and eight radial functions describing pair distances.

Links extend to 5~\AA, where an angstrom (\AA) is $10^{-10}$ metres. Internal features carry scalar numbers and vectors with direction. Rotating a molecule rotates its predicted forces without changing its energy, properties called equivariance for forces and invariance for energy. The energy baseline has 43,521 parameters; models with separate energy and force outputs have 43,649.

The energy baseline calculates $\widehat{\mathbf F}^{E}=-\nabla_{\mathbf R}\widehat E$. Here, $\widehat E$ is predicted energy, $\mathbf R$ contains atom positions, and the gradient gives energy slopes along position coordinates. Both the direct and consistency models also predict energy trained against reference labels, so they are not force-only models. Ordinary direct training has no agreement penalty, while consistency training adds one.
\subsection{Training and Evaluation}\label{sec:training}
The loss combines normalized squared energy error, normalized squared force error and, where applicable, force--energy disagreement; training adjusts parameters to reduce this number:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=w_E\,\mathrm{mean}(e_E^2)+w_F\,\mathrm{mean}(e_F^2)
+\lambda\,\mathrm{mean}\!\left[\left((\widehat{\mathbf F}^{D}-\widehat{\mathbf F}^{E})/s_F\right)^2\right].
\end{equation}
Here, $e_E$ and $e_F$ are errors divided by training-derived scales; $s_F$ is the force scale, and superscript $D$ means direct prediction. Weights $w_E=0.014584365670390719$ and $w_F=1.9854156343296092$ balance supervised errors. The energy baseline omits the final term, while direct training uses $\lambda=0$ and consistency training uses a positive $\lambda$, the consistency strength.

Final E3x fits use Adam, an optimization method that updates parameters, with learning rate 0.003 and 64-bit floating-point arithmetic. Each update samples four records that can repeat, giving 800,000 sampled exposures across 200,000 updates. Two seeds specify random initialization and sampling, providing a limited repeatability check.

Validation occurs every 500 updates, including zero, and BEST is the first saved model state, or checkpoint, with minimum validation force error.

For each validation connection, force mean absolute error (MAE) averages the absolute prediction error across every record, every atom and all three coordinate directions. The reported score averages these five connection scores equally; smaller values are better.

Scores use meV/\AA: a millielectronvolt is one thousandth of an electronvolt, an energy unit, and energy divided by distance measures force.

Recovery additionally requires maximum absolute predicted force component $\leq0.01$~eV/\AA\ and aligned structural RMSD $\leq0.05$~\AA. Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) measures typical atom-position disagreement after rotation, translation and allowed atom-symmetry matching. Small predicted force is a stopping condition; it is not small prediction error.

\subsection{Relaxation Procedure}
The procedure proposes $\mathbf R_{\mathrm{trial}}=\mathbf R+\eta\widehat{\mathbf F}$, where $\eta$ converts predicted forces into coordinate changes. It starts at 0.2~\AA$^2$/eV and can grow to 1.0. Backtracking halves a failed trial's scale; each branch permits 1,500 force attempts.

The procedure checks force convergence first; otherwise, forces pointing broadly in compatible directions can permit acceptance. There is no energy-decrease test or per-atom movement cap, so a scale limit does not cap movement when forces are large.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Baseline Development}
Early E3x training on a 128-record subset plateaued, but MACE, which also derives forces from energy, fitted those records to 0.001515 of the zero-force squared error. Different settings prevent a controlled comparison, but the plateau did not establish an unavoidable data or energy-derived-force limit.

Later 1,000-update energy and direct fits selected update zero as BEST: training improved energy but worsened force predictions.

Three 200,000-update energy baselines were then completed (Figure~\ref{fig:baseline}). A used width 32 and learning rate 0.001; B used width 64 and 0.003. Both used equal normalized energy/force weights, while C retained B's structure and rate but used the balanced weights in Section~\ref{sec:training}. Width counts internal feature channels.

C reached 34.587~meV/\AA, versus 40.370 for B and 42.108 for A, and passed the adequacy rule. Predicting zero force gives 62.504~meV/\AA, so C reduces that error by 44.664\%. However, C's energy error is higher than B's: 126.990 versus 28.178~meV per molecule. Configuration C was selected using the energy baseline; its recipe was transferred without retuning the other model types.
\subsection{Predictive Accuracy and Computational Cost}
Reusing C seed zero and completing nine additional fits provided two seeds per row in Table~\ref{tab:prediction}. Consistency strength 0.1 reduces normalized force--energy disagreement squared error to 0.49--0.61, versus 116--379 for direct training. Its measured fit-plus-score time on CPU is 1.985 times direct prediction, excluding other diagnostics and overhead. This is not inference speed.

Only strength 0.1 meets the declared matching rule against direct prediction: the mean-error gap must be within 10\% of the smaller mean and within both seed spans. A span is maximum minus minimum.

BEST is optimistic because it selects among 401 checks. Over updates 150,000--200,000, the last 50,000 updates, baseline errors vary by 2.2--3.1~meV/\AA\ standard deviation across correlated checks. Nine subsequent fits finish 0.1--7.4~meV/\AA\ above BEST, and the 0.162~meV/\AA\ matching gap is smaller than this variation. Matching does not establish equivalence.

MACE Run A independently scores 5.349~meV/\AA\ with equal connection weighting after 2,048 epochs, or training-data passes. Architecture, exposure and hardware differ; this is an external reference, not a controlled efficiency comparison. MACE recovery remains unmeasured.

\subsection{Structural Recovery}
A relaxation procedure repeatedly moves atoms along predicted forces. Five saved E3x checkpoints were tested from the same ten branch starts across five validation connections. Each branch has its own target, and all six distinct target minima appear in training. Leaving the starts unchanged recovers zero targets, while every evaluated checkpoint also recovers 0/10 (Table~\ref{tab:recovery}). This fails the declared pilot pass rule, so successful recovery cannot rank the methods.

\subsection{Endpoint Diagnostics}
A 155-evaluation check reproduced all 50 stopping flags. Every model exceeds the stopping force threshold at all 11 stored aspirin minima, which include the six targets. The reference labels themselves stay below 2.745~meV/\AA. This mismatch at exact reference shapes does not exclude nearby shapes meeting both tolerances.

All 23 force-converged direct/consistency endpoints have disconnected 5~\AA\ neighbour graphs, with atoms in different fragments beyond the model's interaction cutoff. These are not intact molecular shapes, although internal fragment forces can remain. Only seven endpoints have every atom isolated, where the direct-force output is exactly zero automatically; this does not establish a correct molecular minimum.

Four selected trace replays reproduced the saved endpoints. Two separated, with largest accepted atom movements of 436.721~\AA\ for direct prediction and 268.047~\AA\ for energy-derived forces. The other two remained connected, with maxima 0.086492~\AA\ for consistency and 0.006083~\AA\ for energy-derived forces. The separated energy trace ended without convergence; both force-converged energy endpoints remained connected.

Evidence covers aspirin, five validation connections and two seeds, with shared minima. Validation selects settings and BEST; test generalization is unestablished. Failed and incomplete trials remain recorded.

\subsection{Representation Study}
Feedback from a faculty member, a professor of materials science, suggested comparing models with and without rotation symmetry; the internal-feature check is related but different. E64 is the existing width-64 model with scalar numbers and directional vectors; its two fits were reused. Four new scalar-only fits match its width (S64) or approximate parameter count (SP). All models retain force rotation symmetry, so this is not a no-symmetry comparison. E64 had lower validation force error in both seeds under E64-selected settings, without separate scalar tuning. Findings remain preliminary, await independent interpretation review, and establish neither feature causality nor better recovery.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
These checks expose model and procedure limitations, without proving that the procedure alone caused failures or that real molecular break-up occurred. Better agreement on stored examples cannot guarantee correct shapes, and zero recovery cannot establish method equivalence.

\paragraph{Changes from the proposal.}
Two seeds were completed rather than the proposed three, and paracetamol replication is deferred until aspirin recovery is feasible. A revised adequacy protocol followed the preserved small-subset failures; responsibilities changed, and the representation check adds an extension. The models use a 5~\AA\ cutoff; faculty suggested studying interactions beyond 7~\AA\ as future work. These results do not show that missing long-range interactions caused the failures.
\paragraph{Planned Work.}
First, verify the MACE loader against saved predictions and run the unchanged ten-start and six-reference controls. Second, use these controls to assess a separately declared per-atom movement cap on the same checkpoints and starts, labelling reused-validation analysis as post-hoc.

Third, measure closed-loop work and prediction time on identical hardware, declaring any matched-wall-time training budget before new runs. Work adds products of matching force and displacement components around a closed path; forces from one smooth energy have an exact zero closed-loop integral. Numerical integration can introduce residual error.
