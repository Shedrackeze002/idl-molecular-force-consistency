# Analytical energy-offset profiling: methods draft

This note records the fixed method used in the controlled aspirin diagnostic. The trial is still running when this draft is saved, so it describes the method and its acceptance rules, not a completed learning result. The reviewed specification and implementation remain the authority for exact settings.

We separate the atomic energy-head bias from the other model parameters. Every fitted structure in this diagnostic has 21 atoms, so its predicted residual energy can be written as E_i(theta, b) = f_i(theta) + 21b. The shared bias does not depend on atomic coordinates. Its contribution to the predicted force, which is the negative energy gradient, is therefore zero.

For a fixed theta, let e_i = f_i(theta) + 21b - y_i. The fitting-set least-squares optimum follows directly: delta_b = -mean_fit(e_i)/21. We apply this one correction over all 128 ordered fitting examples. The 16 anchors are fitted examples too, and we evaluate them with that same model bias. We do not recalibrate the anchors or individual connection groups.

At the optimum, the pooled fitting energy loss is its centered population variance. If s_E and s_F denote the original normalization scales and w_E and w_F the fixed loss weights, the profiled objective is w_E Var_fit(f_i(theta)-y_i)/s_E^2 + w_F MSE_fit(F(theta), F_target)/s_F^2. The derivative of the fitted mean cancels because the centered residuals sum to zero. This gives the envelope derivative of the globally profiled objective by evaluating the original raw weighted derivative at the calibrated incoming model.

We accumulate the raw derivative across eight 16-example chunks, all evaluated at the same incoming parameters. Centering each chunk separately would define a different objective because it would remove differences between chunk means. The engineering witness makes this distinction explicit: residuals [-3, -1, 1, 3] have global variance 5, while independently centering the two chunks would give variance 1. A separate real-model test uses unequal chunks and a nonzero readout, so the check is not limited to this scalar illustration.

Only the scalar bias derivative is masked after we verify its unmasked magnitude is at most 1e-10. Its Adam moments remain exactly zero while the common Adam count advances. The remaining parameters receive the unchanged Adam update at learning rate 0.0003, with weights w_E = 0.014584365670390719 and w_F = 1.9854156343296092. We then recompute all fitting energies and apply the outgoing bias correction before accepting the state. This changes the optimization path; it is more than a final reporting adjustment. Although a bias shift alone leaves forces unchanged at fixed theta, the resulting body-parameter path can change force accuracy, and that outcome must be measured.

The trial restores the saved historical width32 update-0 state without calling a parameter initializer. We preserve the original checkpoint bytes and separately verify canonical parameter, optimizer and full-state identities. The first calibration creates a distinct projected update-0 state. Learning gates continue to use the original unprojected energy and force MSE baselines on both fitted roles. All four final MSE conditions must be strictly below 0.1 times those baselines. We do not replace the denominator with the lower projected update-0 energy error.

The fixed trial requests 6,000 accepted updates and 6,001 accepted calibrations, including the initial correction. It saves 63 scored states and compares 62 matching postzero states with the completed historical width32 run. One discarded warmup proposal and two independent reference proposals are counted separately. At outgoing updates 1 and 3001, the numerical oracle differentiates the full-support centered objective, applies independent NumPy Adam from the actual incoming moments, independently corrects the reference bias, and compares gradients, parameters, moments and energy/force predictions. A single fresh process must reproduce all 144 endpoint role entries without training or random initialization.

We keep the distinction between numerical validity and learning success. Mean-zero pooled fitting energy is imposed by construction, so it cannot establish learned calibration or generalization. Centered energy variation, raw force error and group-level errors remain empirical. All examples here are fitted, the comparison uses one seed and a historical reference, and the sparse score grid cannot establish continuous stability or convergence. Dense correction receipts also have no matching dense historical trajectory. Failed calls, interrupted work, failed verification and negative learning outcomes remain part of the evidence trail.

Eliminating a linear nuisance parameter has a general precedent in variable projection. Golub and Pereyra's [1973 publisher abstract](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0710036) describes separable nonlinear least-squares problems with linear coefficients. We derive and test the fixed-atom scalar-bias case directly here. This reference supplies methodological background; it does not demonstrate a benefit for this MLIP experiment. The full publisher text was access-gated and its link was supplied for CMU access.
