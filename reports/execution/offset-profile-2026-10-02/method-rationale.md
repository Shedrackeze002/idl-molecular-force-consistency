# Why a scalar bias can be profiled

The frozen model predicts E_i(theta,b)=E_body,i(theta)+n b and F_i(theta,b)=−dE_body,i/dR for this constant-composition support (n=21). For positive energy weight a, the bias derivative of the weighted scaled raw energy loss is2 a n mean(E−target)/s_E². Its unique scalar optimum is b*=mean(target−E_body)/n; force loss is independent of b. Substitution gives a Var(E_body−target)/s_E² plus the unchanged weighted raw force loss.

In the centered-energy derivative, the extra derivative of the mean multiplies a sum of centered residuals equal to zero. The derivative with respect to theta therefore equals the raw full-support derivative evaluated at b*, as the envelope rule suggests. This equivalence requires full-support calibration, not separate microbatch means. Numerical tolerances, finite precision and nonzero incoming optimizer moments still need actual verification. Varying atom counts would require a different weighted scalar least-squares formula; this trial rejects that case.

Variable projection is a standard approach for eliminating linear coefficients in separable nonlinear least squares, documented in Golub and Pereyra (1973), [The Differentiation of Pseudo-Inverses and Nonlinear Least Squares Problems Whose Variables Separate](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0710036).
